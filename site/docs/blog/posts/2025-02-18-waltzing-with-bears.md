# Waltzing with Bears: Embracing Risk Instead of Ignoring It

Tom DeMarco and Timothy Lister’s _Waltzing with Bears_ tackles an uncomfortable truth in project management: **most projects fail because leaders pretend risk doesn’t exist.** Instead of planning for uncertainty, teams act as if everything will go according to plan—only to be blindsided when reality hits.

<!-- more -->

The book isn’t about avoiding risk—it’s about acknowledging, managing, and even **leveraging** it to make smarter decisions. Whether in software, engineering, or leadership, **risk is unavoidable**. The companies and teams that succeed aren’t the ones who ignore it but the ones who prepare for it.

---

## Key Lessons from _Waltzing with Bears_

### 1\. The Most Dangerous Risks Are the Ones You Ignore

- Many organisations operate under the illusion of certainty.
- If your project plan has no room for risk, it’s not a real plan—it’s wishful thinking.
- The earlier you identify risks, the better your chances of mitigating them.

💡 **Practical takeaway:** Every project has risks. Pretending they don’t exist doesn’t make them go away.

### 2\. Risk Management Is More Than Just a Checklist

- Many companies treat risk management as a bureaucratic exercise—filling out forms but not actually preparing for real risks.
- The best teams constantly reassess and adapt based on new information.
- Effective risk management is about **decision-making, not documentation.**

💡 **Practical takeaway:** A risk plan no one follows is as useless as no plan at all.

### 3\. Some Risks Are Worth Taking

- Not all risks are bad—some are necessary for growth and innovation.
- The right kind of risk can create opportunities that wouldn’t exist otherwise.
- The key is to **understand the potential downsides and prepare accordingly.**

💡 **Practical takeaway:** Smart leaders take **calculated risks**, not blind leaps.

### 4\. Planning for Risk Improves Delivery, Not Slows It Down

- Teams often resist risk planning because they think it adds "unnecessary complexity."
- In reality, **ignoring risk leads to blown budgets, missed deadlines, and last-minute chaos.**
- A project with built-in risk buffers is far more likely to succeed than one running on blind optimism.

💡 **Practical takeaway:** A project with no contingency plan is a project that’s doomed to fail.

### 5\. Leaders Must Be Honest About Uncertainty

- Many managers demand "accurate" estimates when the reality is full of unknowns.
- Good leaders don’t push for fake certainty—they acknowledge risk and make informed decisions.
- Transparency about uncertainty builds trust and credibility within teams.

💡 **Practical takeaway:** Saying "I don’t know, but here’s how we’ll handle it" is better than making up numbers.

---

## Why _Waltzing with Bears_ Still Matters

Most failures in software, product development, and leadership aren’t caused by **technical** problems—they happen because people refuse to acknowledge uncertainty.

_Waltzing with Bears_ is a reminder that **risk isn’t the enemy—unpreparedness is.** If you want to avoid disasters, stop pretending you can predict everything. Instead, **plan for the unknown and give your team the tools to adapt.**

Because in the real world, every project is a dance with risk. And the best leaders know how to waltz.
