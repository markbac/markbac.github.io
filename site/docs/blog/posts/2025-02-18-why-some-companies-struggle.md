# Why Some Companies Struggle with Software Development (When They Really Shouldn’t)

It’s always baffling when you see a company—well-funded, packed with smart people—**consistently struggling to deliver software effectively**. We’re not talking about under-resourced startups here; these are **established organisations** that somehow keep making the same mistakes.

<!-- more -->

This isn’t because the knowledge isn’t out there. We have **decades of best practices, frameworks, and proven methodologies** to guide software development. The real issue? Many companies either **don’t realise they exist, dismiss them as unnecessary, or think they can cut corners**.

The result?

* **Inefficiency and slow delivery** 🚶‍♂️💨
* **Mounting technical debt** 🏗️💸
* **Demoralised teams** 😩
* **Software that’s a nightmare to maintain and scale** 🤯

And the worst part? **It’s all preventable.**

***

## The Leadership Gap

One of the biggest reasons companies get software development wrong is **misalignment at the leadership level**.

Decisions about **how software should be built** are often made by **people without a software engineering background**. They might be great leaders in other areas, but **software development isn’t something you can “figure out as you go”**. It requires an understanding of engineering principles, technical debt, and long-term maintainability.

Would you put a **chef in charge of a manufacturing plant**? No. But in software, it happens **all the time**.

### How This Plays Out

* Leadership focuses on **deadlines over quality**, treating software like a production line rather than an evolving system.
* They **throw tools at problems** instead of fixing the root causes—leading to bloated JIRA boards, endless meetings, and an illusion of control.
* They **don’t invest in foundational engineering practices** (CI/CD, test automation, architecture reviews), leading to a house of cards **that collapses under its own weight**.

### The Fix

- ✔ **Hire or consult experienced engineering leaders** who understand how to **balance delivery with long-term sustainability**.
- ✔ **Trust technical experts**—let engineers shape the software processes, not just project managers and execs.
- ✔ **Measure success beyond just “on-time delivery”**—prioritise maintainability, scalability, and developer experience.

***

## Short-Term Wins, Long-Term Costs ⏳

Many companies fall into the **trap of short-term thinking**.

They rush to deliver **the first release, then the next**—momentum feels great… **until it doesn’t**.

Over time, cracks appear:

* Every **new feature breaks something else**.
* Deployments become **painful and risky**.
* Development **slows to a crawl** because no one understands how the system actually works anymore.

By prioritising **immediate speed over sustainable development**, these companies **doom themselves to long-term failure**.

### How to Avoid This Trap

- ✅ Ask **“What will this decision cost us a year from now?”** before cutting corners.
- ✅ Establish **engineering standards early**—CI/CD, automated testing, and clean architecture **pay off massively in the long run**.
- ✅ Shift focus from **“just ship it” to “ship it sustainably”**.

***

## Culture Over Process

A company’s **culture** plays a far bigger role in software success than any **framework or methodology**.

A **command-and-control culture**, where **decisions are made top-down** without engineering input, **kills innovation and efficiency**. Developers become order-takers, and any sense of ownership disappears.

On the flip side, companies that **value learning, autonomy, and iteration** thrive. They **don’t just “do Agile” or “do DevOps” for the sake of it**—they actually **embrace** continuous improvement.

### The Signs of a Dysfunctional Culture

- ❌ Leadership **dictates solutions** instead of working with engineers to find the best approach.
- ❌ Developers **aren’t trusted to make technical decisions**—everything is micro-managed.
- ❌ Failure is **punished, not learned from**, leading to a risk-averse and innovation-stifling environment.

### The Fix

- ✔ **Give teams autonomy**—let engineers **own their solutions** rather than just implementing top-down decisions.
- ✔ **Encourage experimentation and learning**—mistakes should **inform better processes, not create fear**.
- ✔ **Build a feedback loop**—regular retrospectives, architecture reviews, and learning sessions should be **part of the process, not an afterthought**.

***

## The Talent Trap

Great engineers **want to build great software**. If they join a company **full of dysfunction and resistance to change**, they’ll try to **fix it**.

If leadership listens, **things improve**.

If leadership **ignores them or pushes back**, they leave. And when they do, they take **knowledge, experience, and potential solutions with them**.

**The revolving door begins.**

### The Talent Drain Effect

1️⃣ **Experienced engineers leave** because they’re tired of fighting the same battles.\
2️⃣ The company replaces them with **less experienced hires**, who inherit the same broken processes.\
3️⃣ Without strong mentorship, **bad practices get reinforced** instead of improved.\
4️⃣ More engineers leave. **Rinse and repeat.**

### How to Stop It

- ✅ **Create an environment where experienced engineers want to stay.**
- ✅ **Listen to their concerns and act on them.**
- ✅ **Invest in mentorship and internal growth**—build a system where **people level up instead of burning out**.

***

## The Fix: It’s Not Rocket Science

The good news? **These problems are fixable.**

- ✅ **Admit there’s an issue** – Leadership needs to acknowledge when software development is broken and **be open to change**.
- ✅ **Hire the right people** – Bring in **experienced engineering leaders** who understand how to balance **delivery, quality, and maintainability**.
- ✅ **Prioritise sustainable development** – Think **beyond the next release**—ask how today’s decisions **affect the next 5 years**.
- ✅ **Build a culture of continuous improvement** – Not just by **adopting Agile or DevOps**, but by **truly embracing the mindset behind them**.
- ✅ **Trust engineers** – If your best engineers **keep leaving**, **ask why**—and actually listen to their answers.

**Building software well isn’t a mystery.** There’s an entire ecosystem of **patterns, practices, and frameworks** that have been tested over decades. The companies that **get it right** are the ones that **embrace them**—instead of **reinventing the wheel (or worse, ignoring the wheel altogether)**.

If your organisation feels stuck, **stop throwing more process at the problem**.

Take a step back. **Ask the hard questions.** Bring in the right people. **Build a culture that values learning, sustainable development, and continuous improvement.**

🚀 Do that, and you’ll be amazed at **how quickly things turn around.**
