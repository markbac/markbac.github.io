# Managing Humans: The No-Nonsense Guide to Engineering Leadership

Michael Lopp’s _Managing Humans_ is not your typical management book. It’s not filled with corporate jargon, pointless frameworks, or idealistic theories. Instead, it’s a brutally honest, often hilarious collection of stories and lessons from the trenches of tech leadership.

<!-- more -->

Lopp (better known by his blog pseudonym, "Rands") has spent decades managing engineers at places like Apple, Slack, and Netscape. His core message? **Managing humans is messy, unpredictable, and requires more than just technical know-how.** If you think leadership is about wielding authority, enforcing structure, or driving people like machines, you’re doing it wrong.

---

## Key Lessons from _Managing Humans_

### 1\. Managing Engineers is Different from Managing Other Professions

- Engineers **hate pointless meetings, bureaucracy, and micromanagement.**
- They care about **problem-solving, autonomy, and well-defined challenges.**
- If you treat engineers like interchangeable parts, **they’ll check out—or leave.**

💡 **Practical takeaway:** Understand what motivates engineers (hint: it’s not just money).

### 2\. The "Bored People Quit" Rule

- Talented engineers **don’t stick around if they aren’t learning.**
- Keeping them engaged means giving them **interesting, meaningful problems to solve.**
- If you let them stagnate, they’ll find somewhere else to grow.

💡 **Practical takeaway:** Challenge your team—don’t just keep them busy.

### 3\. Leadership is About Trust, Not Authority

- People don’t follow titles—they follow leaders who earn their trust.
- Trust is built through **honest communication, consistency, and fairness.**
- If your team doesn’t trust you, **they won’t tell you when things are going wrong.**

💡 **Practical takeaway:** If you want honest feedback, **be the kind of leader people feel safe talking to.**

### 4\. Your Job is Not to Solve Everything

- Many first-time managers fail because they try to **do everything themselves.**
- Good managers delegate, coach, and empower their teams.
- If you’re constantly firefighting, **you’re not actually leading.**

💡 **Practical takeaway:** Step back, trust your team, and focus on the bigger picture.

### 5\. Politics Exist—Ignore Them at Your Peril

- Even in engineering-focused companies, **office politics exist.**
- Understanding power dynamics helps you **protect your team from nonsense.**
- Managing up (dealing with your boss and execs) is just as important as managing down.

💡 **Practical takeaway:** Don’t pretend politics don’t exist—learn to navigate them **without selling your soul.**

---

## Why _Managing Humans_ Still Matters

Unlike traditional management books, _Managing Humans_ is **practical, funny, and brutally honest.** It’s the kind of book that speaks directly to **technical leaders, engineers-turned-managers, and anyone who wants to survive in tech leadership without losing their sanity.**

If you lead engineers, this book will help you avoid common mistakes, earn your team’s respect, and build a culture where smart people actually want to work.

Because at the end of the day, **managing humans isn’t about systems or processes—it’s about understanding people.**
